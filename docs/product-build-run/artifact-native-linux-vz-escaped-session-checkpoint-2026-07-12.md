## Outcome

The fourth teardown-stress gate proves that cgroup-backed teardown still reaches an unprivileged
fixture after it escapes its launcher's session and process group. After exec, the fixture calls
`setsid()` and sends a bounded readiness record binding its PID, prior session and process group,
and new session and process-group IDs. The protected launcher corroborates the record through
procfs, live `getsid()` and `getpgid()` observations, package credentials, and cgroup membership
before starting a guest-monotonic one-second deadline.

At the deadline the launcher proves that the same process remains the new session and process-group
leader in the measured cgroup. It then delivers exactly one `SIGTERM`, reaps that child as signal
15, and requires strict protected fork/exec/setsid/TERM/exit order. Only afterward does it prove no
descendant remains, close every sensor, empty and remove the cgroup, and emit the canonical record.
Rust and Swift independently require the exact session target, signal, lineage, cleanup, health,
heartbeat, and zero-drop fields and reject forged or noncanonical variants.
